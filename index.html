After the session

You write notes the way you always do. The wiki keeps up.

Nothing changes in your wiki until you accept it. Every proposed edit shows what it changes and why.

  1. Paste your notes

    Shorthand, typos, half-sentences. Names get matched to pages you already have.

  2. Review the changes

    Updates to existing pages, new NPCs saved as drafts, and a question when something is ambiguous.

  3. Prep the next session

    Loose threads, who's likely to show up, and what each of them wants, on one screen.

What's inside

Built for the DM who's twelve sessions in and losing track.

A wiki that links itself

NPCs, locations, factions, items, quests, and lore, connected with [[links]] the way you'd write them by hand.

Answers with sources

Ask about your world and every answer names the pages and sessions it came from. If it isn't in your notes, it says so.

Drafts stay drafts

Generated NPCs, encounters, and descriptions are marked as drafts until you make them canon.

Next-session prep

Open threads, NPCs likely to appear, and who owes whom, pulled together before game night.

Portraits and encounters

Generate a portrait or build an encounter from an NPC's page, using the details you've already written.

Secrets, enforced

DM-only notes are kept out of the player journal and out of any answer a player can see.

Your files

Your world is plain markdown. Take it anywhere.

Every page is a markdown file you can export to Obsidian or any editor. Leaving DM Companion never means losing your campaign.
